0.7.1 -- unreleased


THE TAKEOVER STOPS DOING EVERYTHING ON ONE FRAME.

The whole thing ran on a single 700ms clock, so nine frames out of ten it cost
nothing at all and the tenth did twenty subsystems back to back: forty cars
talked down, sixty people looked at, the parking, the pushing, the chatter, the
fireworks, the helicopter. Every one of them is cheap on its own. The pile of
them landing in one frame is a stutter you can set your watch by, which is not
the same thing as a mod being slow.

Same beat, work spread across it. Three things stay on every tick because a
delay is visible in them -- the traffic, the cars going round, the crowd being
held to its ring -- and the other seventeen take turns over three ticks. Nothing
runs less often than it needs to: each already carries its own clock measured in
tens of seconds, and all this changes is which tick it gets to ask.

AND IT NOW TELLS YOU WHAT IT COST. The heaviest tick of the night goes in the
log with the slice that did it and what was on the street at the time, on new
peaks only. The stagger above is reasoning about where the cost must be, not a
measurement of where it is; this is the measurement.

AN EMPTY CAR IN THE CIRCLE GOES, OURS OR NOT.

The test used to spare anything the takeover had put there, and that was leaving
cars exactly where the rule exists to stop them being. Everything it parks is
"ours" -- the whole ring of spectators, the drivers waiting a turn -- so a
spectator nudged onto the tarmac by somebody's donut, or a performer whose driver
was shot out of it, sat there empty and nothing ever touched it again.

Your own car is never taken. That guard only covered the one you are sitting in,
which was enough while the circle only removed cars with somebody at the wheel
and stopped being enough the moment an empty one started going -- park on the
tarmac to watch and you have made exactly that car. The one he is in, the one he
last got out of, and the register of cars he has bought.


THE ONES CARRYING A BEER DRINK IT WHILE THEY WALK.

Until now the only time a bottle in somebody's hand did anything was at a stop:
the crew planted their feet, the ones with a beer took a pull, and they set off
again. So a man carried a beer the whole length of Grove Street and only ever
drank it standing still, which is not what anybody does with a beer -- you drink
it as you go, and you stop for other reasons.

The game already has the clips. amb@code_human_wander_drinking is what its own
wandering drinkers use and it is authored for exactly this: a swig taken
mid-stride with the walk carrying on underneath. All three idles in the male set
are used, checked against the game's own dump rather than remembered.

Rolled PER MAN, every fifteen to forty seconds, rather than per crew -- three
lads with three beers drinking in unison is a dance number. Never while one of
them is stood still, so it does not collide with the stop, and never in the first
stretch after a bottle is handed out.


THE BODY'S CARD SAYS AFRICAN AMERICAN.

Every other line on that card is written the way an official one would write it
-- a date in full, a height in feet and inches, an affiliation or None -- and the
ethnicity was the one field written the way a witness statement would. The rest
of the list was already in that register: Hispanic, Middle Eastern, South Asian.

One constant rather than five string literals, because it appears in the model
table and in the fallback roll, and a rename that catches one and not the other
is a card that says two things about the same man on two different days.


THE ONLINE PEDESTRIANS STOP LOSING THEIR LEGS.  [Five0 Patrol]

SET_PED_RANDOM_COMPONENT_VARIATION picks an index per clothing slot out of what
the MODEL declares it has -- and an Online model in singleplayer declares
drawables whose geometry lives in a DLC pack the streamer has not mounted for an
ambient pedestrian. The index is perfectly legal and the mesh is simply not
there, so the slot renders as nothing: a man from the waist up, walking down
Strawberry.

It looks like a spawn height or a collision problem and it is neither.

The shipped default combination is the one every model guarantees, so it goes on
first as the floor; then the random pass on top; then every slot is asked
IS_PED_COMPONENT_VARIATION_VALID -- the native for exactly this question -- and
put back to the default where the answer is no. Counting the drawables would not
have caught it: the count is what the model declares, and a declared drawable
whose asset is not mounted passes a count check and renders as a hole.


FRANKLIN CAN SAY HELLO TO ANYBODY, AND SAY MORE THAN HELLO TO ANYBODY TWICE.

Walk up to a person, press the context key: he says hey, they say hey back,
nobody stops walking. Press it on the same person again and it turns into a
conversation -- they square up, he opens with whatever he actually makes of
somebody like them, they answer, and it runs six lines before both of them say
goodbye.

NOT ONE WORD OF IT IS WRITTEN. Every line is a speech name out of the game's own
banks, played on the ped's own voice through the game's own ambient speech
system -- so it is lip-synced, it is in the right accent, and a Vagos, a cop and
a stockbroker say three completely different things for nothing, because
Rockstar already decided what each of them sounds like.

AND IT IS THEIR CONVERSATION RATHER THAN HIS. CHAT_STATE and CHAT_RESP are the
game's own pavement chat -- what one pedestrian says to another when the pair of
them stop -- and they are whole sentences, different for every voice in the
game. So a tramp tells you a tramp's story and a woman on Rockford Hills tells
you hers, and nothing here had to write either one. He says hello, they talk, he
says yeah, they talk some more, and both of them say goodbye.

NOBODY IS PROVOKED. Franklin's voice also carries a greeting for a Balla, a
Vago, a cop, a tramp, a junkie and a hillbilly, and picking the right one for
whoever was stood there is a lovely thing that produces the wrong scene: most of
those are not greetings, they are what he makes of somebody he has just clocked,
and he delivers them like it. The one kept is his greeting to his own set, which
is the only warm one among them.

NO PROMPT EITHER. Every other context action in the mod is somewhere -- a boot,
a counter, a door -- and a box in the corner is how you find out it is there.
This one is anywhere there is a person, so a prompt for it would be a box on the
screen permanently. It still stands down silently when something with a real
prompt has spoken for the key: a shop door you are stood in beats the pedestrian
walking past it.


YOU STOP TRIPPING WHEN THE BARS WEAR OFF.

The stumble is the game's own loose-balance flag, and it was switched on with the
CAMERA SHAKE and never switched off until the whole thing tore down. The comedown
shakes too -- so for the fifteen seconds after the ticker said the xanax had worn
off, you were still flagged drunk and still going over, on a screen that had told
you it was finished.

It follows the WALK now. You stumble while you are walking like a drunk and stop
when you are walking like a man who has been up all night, which is what the
comedown's gait already was.

Written only when it changes, because it is a state flag on the ped and another
mod on the same machine writes the same one -- Bare Minimum sets it when you are
exhausted. Two writers on one flag is survivable; two both hammering it every
frame is not.

And a high that ends puts your walk back whatever it thinks it did to you. The
reset was guarded by this file's own record of what it had applied -- a record it
only writes when a clipset actually streams in, so the one case where you most
need your legs back was the case that skipped it. It also drops the wardrobe's
latch on the way out: clearing a movement clipset clears whichever one is on him,
including the walk you chose, and without that the first high of a session took
it away for the rest of the session.


SCENE PROPS INDOORS STOP FLICKERING.

AN OBJECT A SCRIPT MAKES BELONGS TO NO ROOM. The game culls what is inside an
interior through its portals -- doorways and windows -- and to do that every
entity in there has to be assigned to a room. One created by a script is assigned
to none, so the portal system has no answer for it and settles the question
differently from one frame to the next: drawn, culled, drawn.

That is the flicker exactly, and it is why the six scenes before Denise's kitchen
never showed it. Every one of them is outdoors.

The room is read off the thing itself once it exists -- it is standing at the
coordinate, so the game can say which interior and which room that is -- and then
forced, which is the difference between happening to resolve there and being
there. Nothing at all happens to a prop in the open air.

And the draw distance comes out of the file now. Menyoo writes one per placement
and this had never read it, so everything got whatever a script object is handed
by default, which for a carton on a worktop is short enough to blink as you cross
the kitchen. Honoured up to a ceiling, because a hundred props each insisting on
being drawn from two kilometres away is a bill somebody pays in frames.


ECSTASY HAS ITS OWN ICON: TWO PRESSED TABLETS, A SMILEY AND A HEART.

It was two capsules, which is a picture of a prescription -- it is what the
painkiller this slot briefly held actually looks like -- and it was doing no work
at all to tell you which of the two pill products you were holding, because the
bar next to it is also a white pressed thing.

The face is PUNCHED rather than drawn: the stamp on a pressed pill is a recess,
and a recess in this icon set is a hole that shows the panel through. Drawing it
in a tone instead would make the face a sticker. It is the same thing the score
lines on the Xanax bar do.

A smiley on one and a heart on the other, rather than the same stamp twice: two
identical marks read as one mark printed twice, and the whole job of the second
tablet is to say there is more than one thing in the bag. A heart is also still a
heart at the size a face stops being a face.

And each tablet has a SIDE WALL. A disc with a bevel on it is a coin -- it has no
thickness, and thickness is the one feature that says pill. The wall is a second
disc set down by a fifth of the radius with the face laid on top of it, so what
shows underneath is a crescent of edge. The two sit across from each other rather
than one behind the other, because a tablet mostly hidden behind another tablet
has no silhouette of its own.

And a personal vehicle's mark goes away while you are driving it. A blip on the
car you are in sits exactly under the arrow that already says where you are --
two pictures on one spot, one of them telling you something you could not fail
to know. It comes straight back when you get out.


HIS CAR IS ON THE MAP FROM ANYWHERE ON IT NOW, AND YOU CAN SEE IT.

Two things were wrong and they compounded.

A BLIP ON A CAR ONLY EXISTS WHILE THE CAR DOES, and a car three streets away has
been streamed out. So the mark on his Buffalo appeared when you were close enough
to see the car anyway and vanished the moment you were not, which is the exact
opposite of what a map is for -- you do not open the map to find the thing in
front of you.

There are two marks now and never both. While it is loaded it wears one of its
own that moves with it; the moment it streams out, a standing mark goes down on
the spot it was last seen and holds the place until it is seen again.

AND IT WAS DARK GREY AT TWO THIRDS ALPHA. The distinction that was drawing is a
fair one -- a car he was given should not wear the blue of a car he bought off
Hao -- but dark grey against a grey map is not a subtle mark, it is an absent
one. Green: Franklin's own colour in this game, which says "his" more exactly
than grey said "not bought", and at full alpha.

His bike had the identical hole and gets the identical fix.


SOMEBODY WALKING OVER TO BUY WEARS A BLIP.

The corner picks a plausible customer off the street and sends him to you, and
from where you are standing that is one pedestrian out of nine walking roughly
your way -- which is what pedestrians do. There was no telling the man coming to
buy from the man going to the shop, so the first you knew of a sale was somebody
arriving in front of you.

276, a dollar sign, green, short range: a thing on your minimap while you are
stood on your corner rather than an arrow across the map. It is on the PED and
not the spot, which is the opposite of what the headstones do and for the
opposite reason -- a body's blip is about where, a customer's is about who. If
he is cleared mid-approach it goes with him, which is right: nobody is coming.

And the bag sits lower in his hand, and closer in to him.

It hangs off the wrist bone rather than the prop helper the game puts held
objects on, which is why it needs an offset at all -- the wrist is half a hand
further out than where the fingers close. The nudge is now three numbers in the
ini, because bone-local axes are not something anybody can reason about from a
text editor: which way is "down" depends on how that wrist is rotated at that
frame of that animation. Nudge one, reload, look.


THE BIKES AT THE COURTS RIDE ROUND EACH OTHER NOW.

A carload pulling up at a kerb is a carload pulling up at a kerb -- that is what
they came to do. Four men who rode to the Chamberlain courts together do not sit
on stationary bikes for forty seconds looking at each other, and that is what
they were doing, because the hanging-about code was written for the car.

A bike that arrives at a hangout now rides a tight lap -- six to nine metres,
its own radius and its own place on it, so four of them are four circles rather
than one queue -- and every so often somebody holds it on the front brake and
fills the place with smoke.

The lap is POINTS, not a steering lock. A car does a circle by holding full lock
with the grip cut; a bike given the same treatment falls over. So a rider is
handed the next point on his circle every second and a bit and rides between
them. The burnout IS a lock, because that one works on two wheels.

ECSTASY IS ECSTASY AGAIN, and it is a roll rather than a painkiller.

The name and the tag go back -- the id was always "ecstasy", so nothing in any
save moves. And the high has been rebuilt around what the drug actually is.

It was half damage, healing as you walk, a drunk gait and a soft sway, and every
one of those numbers was about NOT feeling things. A roll is the opposite. The
colour warps and slides now, at six tenths so the world underneath stays
readable -- slightly psychedelic rather than a trip, because the trip is heroin
and this one has to be able to run about in. And it has the wired one's legs, at
1.45, with time left at NORMAL on purpose: every other fast drug in here also
drags the world, which makes you feel quick. Nothing drags here, so the speed is
genuinely yours.

The comedown is long, because a roll's is.


A ON THE PAD TAKES WHAT IS ON A BODY. IT WAS X.

The loot screen borrowed the boot's pair -- jump to move one, jump with sprint
to move them all -- and on a pad that is X, with A as the modifier. So the one
button everybody presses to do the obvious thing did nothing here, and the
obvious thing was on the button next to it.

A takes the one under the cursor and A HELD takes the lot: the same action done
harder, on the same button, with nothing extra to learn and nothing extra to
reach for. The tap fires on release, so a hold does not take one on the way past.
The boot and the house keep their pair, because putting a thing down and taking
a thing are two different actions there.

THE BODY IS A HIM OR A HER, AND THE SCREEN NOW KNOWS WHICH.

"What was on him", "Nothing on him", "That's everything he had", "LEAVE HIM" --
all of it was printed over women. The sex was worked out when the body was made
and then thrown away; it is kept now, and every line reads from it.

It is also worked out BETTER. IS_PED_MALE is not reliable and the model name is:
every ped Rockstar ship carries its sex in the middle of its name, and that
convention has no exceptions in the game's own list. A woman on the pavement came
up as Errol Hobbs, six foot five, because the flag said male and nothing else was
asked. The name decides now; the flag is the fallback for an add-on ped.

AND THE CARD IS SET LIKE A CARD.

ETHNICITY was printed through the date of birth -- the label sat exactly where
the value above it ended -- so the gap between lines is now bigger than the thing
that goes in it, which is the only arrangement that cannot collide.

The name is the condensed face in capitals, which is how a name is printed on
every licence anybody has been handed; it was the reading face at a display size,
which is body copy made big. A long one is stepped down to fit rather than cut.
There is a rule under it, which is most of what makes four labels beside a
photograph read as a card. The photograph has an edge, matches the height of the
text beside it, and the labels sit a shade quieter than the answers.

An empty body no longer gets a whole tile of blank panel to say so in.


A SCENE CAN TAKE SOMETHING OUT OF THE WORLD AS WELL AS PUT SOMETHING IN.

The spooner records what you PUT somewhere. There is no tag anywhere in its
format for what you took AWAY -- so a cupboard deleted to make room for a scale
and a set of bags is back next session, standing through the middle of
everything arranged around it. Half of an evening's work saved and half of it
lost, with nothing to say which half.

A scene file may now carry a Removals block, which Menyoo ignores when it loads
one, so a file with removals in it still opens in the spooner and still saves
out of it. Every copy of that model inside a small sphere is hidden while the
scene is up and put back once you are well past. The sphere is small on purpose:
hiding a bin at Denise's must not hide the one outside Gerald's.

Nobody has to write that by hand. Settings, Spooner scenes, "Hide the prop I am
looking at" -- look straight at the thing, press it, and it is gone now and
written into hidden.xml for every session after. It will not take one of a
scene's own props, and it will not take a person or a car.

AND AUNT DENISE'S KITCHEN IS DRESSED FOR WORK. Nineteen props on the counter:
scale, spoon, baking soda, powdered milk, cut, a bag of powder and one of pills;
weed dried, pruned, bagged and piled; acetone, ammonia and acid on the floor
beside it. Seven scenes ship now, 194 placements between them.


HOLD LEFT OR RIGHT AND THE WARDROBE RAIL RUNS.

The closet has always offered every garment the body owns. The counts come
straight from the game, nothing is filtered, and the row says so out loud --
"47 OF 312". What it had no way of doing was reaching the three hundredth of
anything. One press was one garment, so the far end of a rail was three hundred
presses away, and a rail nobody can reach the end of is a short rail with a long
number printed on it.

Slow first and then quick: nine a second after a short hold, winding up over a
second and a half to thirty-five. A single press is still a single garment --
the wait before the first repeat is longer than any tap. The click plays every
fifth step once it is running, because thirty a second is not feedback, it is a
noise.

Only the racks run. Holding right on "Wear it" is not a request for forty
outfits, and on the walks it would flick him through every way of moving in the
game several times a second. Those rows are six long, not six hundred.

AND THE CLOSET NOW WRITES DOWN WHAT IT HAS. Every drawable and every texture the
body owns, counted into the log when the door opens -- twelve component slots
and all eight prop slots, including the three the menu has no row for. "Does it
have all the clothes in it" is not a question anybody can answer by scrolling.


THE PERFORMERS SPIN ON THE SPOT, IN THE MIDDLE, TOGETHER.

Two things were wrong and they were both about where the show is.

The four marks the cars performed on were walked round the junction, and they
are honest -- they are places a car CAN stand at that crossroads. On Carson they
sit between two and a half and fifteen metres from the circle, so one car span
on the centre spot while another was fifteen metres away with its back to it,
and the two did not read as one event. The marks now decide only HOW MANY
perform. Where they perform is an even share of a seven-metre ring round the
middle -- four cars ten metres apart, turned so the set sits on the bearing the
first mark was walked at.

And the donut travelled, because the car was rigged to drift: burnout mode off
and the grip cut to two fifths, which steps the back out and washes the front
wide with it. Lovely on an empty road, wrong in the middle of a ring of people.

Both of those are now reversed. Burnout mode is held ON -- it locks the front
brakes and spins the rears, which is exactly what a driver's left foot does to
hold a car on the spot -- and the grip is left alone, because reduced grip is
what let the front end wash out and the front end washing out IS the travelling.
Drift tyres stay: they loosen the rear, which is the end that should be loose,
and they are where the smoke comes from.

The result is a car pivoting about its front axle. The back swings round, the
nose stays, and it goes nowhere. The leash came in from ten metres to seven with
it, because there are no wide loops left to protect.


THE KITCHEN USES THE GAME'S OWN DRUG-PROCESSOR ANIMATIONS.

Rockstar authored two ambient scenarios for exactly this -- somebody stood at a
table weighing, bagging and cutting -- and the mod was not using either of them.
Every clip it did use was a warehouse press-operator or a maid at a worktop,
borrowed because it had the right SHAPE. Near misses, all of them.

Weed is worked on the weed one and everything else on the coke one, including
meth: the meth clips are a man at a chemistry rig and this is a kitchen counter
in a house, and the near-right animation for the right room beats the right
animation for the wrong one.

CRACK GETS THE BAKING SODA IDLES, which the coke set has a whole second variant
for, and which is what crack is actually cut with.

And it is a SET, not a clip. Each has a base loop and a handful of idles, and
the batch now cycles between them the way the scenario does: he works, he stops
to check a bag, he goes back to it. A single clip on loop is a pose.

The last-resort fallback is now the drug's own scenario, which is these same
animations run by the game -- with the table prop it brings along.


THE GUN COUNTER IS SET IN THREE FACES INSTEAD OF TWO SIZES OF ONE.

The gun's name was the reading face blown up to a display size, and body copy
made big does not become a title -- it becomes big body copy. It is set in
Chalet Comprime Cologne now, the condensed face Rockstar puts weapon names in
on the wheel, at nearly a full unit of scale: tall, tight, and finally the same
language as the game behind it. Names too long for that size are stepped down
to fit rather than run off both edges.

The reading face keeps the two lines that are actually read -- the sentence
somebody wrote about the gun, and the rounds. Everything else is a label, and
labels are condensed.

The rounds line is in capitals like every other label, and its price is in the
money colour. The tones are all a shade brighter: this is the one screen in the
mod with no panel behind it, and the shared dim grey disappeared into a
concrete floor at midday.

THE NUMBER UNDER THE RACK NAME IS THE POSITION NOW.

It used to be the tally -- how many of that rack you already owned -- sat
directly under a rack name, which is the one place on a screen where "5 / 8"
can only be read as the fifth of eight. It never moved as you walked the shelf,
because it was never a position.

Both things are still there. The position is where the eye was already going;
the tally is after it, in words and in green, where it cannot be mistaken for
anything else.


THEY PULL UP, STAND ON IT FOR FIVE SECONDS, AND THEN GO ROUND.

A car used to start its lock on the frame it arrived, so whatever speed it came
in at went straight into the first burst of steering. That is not a donut, it
is a slide -- across the junction, from outside the circle -- and it is why the
performers looked like they were driving through rather than working.

The arrival is three things now and only the last of them is the show. He
brakes onto his mark and actually stops there. He holds a standing burnout for
five seconds, front brake on and the back going, with the crowd up against the
wing. Then the lock comes on from nothing, which is the only way a donut ever
looks like one.

The leash came in from twelve metres to ten at the same time, and it is meant
to bite: a loop that has grown into a lap of the junction gets hauled back to
the middle and started again from a stop.


THREE RINGS ROUND THE TAKEOVER INSTEAD OF TWO.

Out wide, a stranger is asked to go back the way he came, and most drivers do.
At the crowd, the asking stops -- they go for him, he gets out and runs, and
his car stays where it stopped for the rest of the night.

In the circle itself, it goes. That last one is a reversal and it is on
purpose. Nothing here has deleted a car for a long time, because a car
vanishing in front of sixty people reads as a bug. What that did not account
for is the CIRCLE: the ring of tarmac the whole event exists to keep clear. A
car parked in it is not scenery, it is the show cancelled, and there is no
second kerb to send it to because it is already past everything.

So the crowd still gets its go, the driver still walks away from his car, and
only the last thirteen metres -- where nothing may stand -- removes anything.
Never yours, never one of theirs, never anybody with a badge.


STRETCH SELLS NEARLY EVERY GUN IN THE GAME NOW.

Twenty-one pieces across five racks became ninety-five across eight:
handguns, SMGs, shotguns, rifles, snipers, blades, thrown and heavy.

The short shelf was right while the counter was a list you scrolled -- twenty
rows is a screen, a hundred is a chore. It is one gun on a crate now, one at a
time, so length costs nothing to look at, and the only argument left for
keeping it short was that a man in a yard would not have all this. He would.
That is the business.

WHAT IS LEFT OFF is a short list: the joke weapons, the three the game itself
names "Invalid", the rockets that only exist on vehicles, the jerry cans and
the two ray guns. Nothing on it is a gun anybody would buy.

Every weapon id was checked against the mod's own weapons.json as the list was
written, so nothing on that shelf is a name somebody typed from memory.


THE GUN COUNTER LAYS THE GUN ON THE BENCH AND THE CAMERA GOES TO IT.

Eleven of the weapons on that counter had no picture, and it was never going to
be fixed by looking harder. The game ships four weapon-art packs -- and having
swept seventy candidate names on a real install and written down which ones
answered, the AP Pistol, the SNS, the Vintage, the Double Action, the Compact
Rifle, the Double Barrel, the Switchblade, the Machete, the Pipe Bomb and the
Flare are in none of them. Rockstar never made those photographs.

Every one of those weapons has a MODEL, though, because you can hold it. So the
gun you are looking at is laid on its side on the bench in front of Stretch and
the camera comes round to it, which cannot be missing.

THIS IS THE THIRD ARRANGEMENT OF IT and the first that is not fighting the
engine. The first two floated the gun in front of the gameplay camera and cut a
hole in the panel to see it through -- because two dimensional drawing is
composited after the world, so nothing in the room can ever be drawn in front of
a rectangle. That worked, and it was always a workaround: the hole had to be
filled with a painted backdrop, and anything a metre from the camera sits inside
the near depth of field, so the gun came out soft while the shop behind it was
sharp.

A bench answers all of it at once. The gun lies on a real table in real light,
the panel moves aside instead of being cut open, and the camera is ours -- so it
is pointed from a fixed spot at a distance the game is happy to focus at.

AND IT WEARS WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT. Not just the parts you have bought: the
one under the cursor goes on as you scroll to it and comes off as you scroll
away. The question a parts list is actually asking is "what does it look like
with that on", and now the answer is in front of you before you pay.

WHERE THE BENCH IS, IS IN THE INI, because a coordinate can only honestly come
from somebody who has stood on it. The shipped one is a guess an arm's length in
front of him at about table height: put a table down, stand where the middle of
its top is, and write down what the settings screen tells you. The camera is not
a setting -- it is worked out from the bench, because a camera position is the
one thing nobody can judge while standing still.

The photograph is still used where the game has one. It is lit, and it is not
spinning.


THE SPECTATORS PARK QUICKLY NOW, AND NOTHING IS EVER PUT ANYWHERE.

The last one used to arrive a minute and a half after the first, and it spent
most of that stopped in a lane with the others queued behind it. Four things
were doing it.

IT WAITED NINETY SECONDS BEFORE DECIDING A CAR WAS NOT GOING TO MAKE IT. That
number was set when this path ended in the car being SET on its kerb, and a
minute and a half of trying was the price of not teleporting one that was
merely slow. It has not ended that way for a while -- it ends in a different
kerb or in driving off -- so the wait was buying nothing. A car that has not
covered two hundred metres in half a minute is not going to. Twenty-eight
seconds.

IT WOULD ONLY GO ELSEWHERE IF SOMEBODY WAS PROVABLY PARKED ON ITS KERB. But a
kerb can be unreachable without being occupied -- a skip in the way, a fence, a
lorry across the entrance, a bit of road the game will not route to. None of
those look like "taken", so the car stood in the road until the timer fired.
Nine seconds stopped is now enough on its own, whatever is doing it.

IT DROVE THERE AT THIRTY-TWO MILES AN HOUR, from up to two hundred and thirty
metres away. That is twelve now. The crawl among the parked cars is untouched
at walking pace, which is why it is safe to do -- nothing crashes on an
approach, and these drivers already steer around vehicles, people and objects
at full ability.

AND IT GETS SIX GOES at finding a free kerb before it gives up and leaves,
rather than four.

THE SPRAY CAPS ARE DRAWN FROM THE FRONT NOW, AND THEY LOOK LIKE CAPS.

They were drawn from ABOVE -- a tall rounded body with the stem standing up out
of the top of it -- which is the silhouette of an aerosol can whatever the
little ellipse on top was meant to be. Nobody looks at a cap from above. You
look at the face of it, which is the part with the hole in.

So all three are the real object now: a body wider at the top than the bottom,
ribbed down the face with the ribs following the taper, the nozzle punched out
of the middle with the spray hole drawn back into it, and the stem hanging
below. The taper and the stem underneath are what stop it being a can at any
size, and there is no flared skirt -- the bowed bottom edge is the skirt, and
the ellipse that was under it gave the cap a saucer to stand on.

AND THE OVERSPRAY TILE WEARS ONE. There used to be two drawings of the same
object, and the only reason the second existed was that a phone tile forced its
art into a square -- a cap is two thirds as wide as it is tall, so the real one
came out squat and stretched, and rather than fix the tile somebody drew a cap
shaped like a tile. Tiles take a shape now, so the app shows a fat cap: the one
with the biggest nozzle, which is the only part of a cap that survives being
drawn at eighteen pixels.

THE BALACLAVA WAS BEING DRAWN INSIDE OUT. Four attempts at it came out as a
grey alien and every one of them got blamed on something else -- the eye holes
were made into slits, the chin was widened, the whole head was reproportioned --
and none of it helped, because the fault was the polarity. Every reference for
one of these is a BLACK mask on white paper. Icons here are white ink laid on a
dark panel, so filling the silhouette makes the mask white, and a white
head-shaped blob with two slits and a mouth in it is a grey alien whatever you
do to it.

It is outlined now, with the openings filled. The mask is dark, the hood's edge
is a bright line round it, and the eyes and mouth are bright marks in the
middle -- which is the reference translated into this medium rather than copied
into it. The eyes are also nearly level: sloping them down toward the nose is
the angry V a cartoon draws for eyebrows, and that was the other half of it.

AND THE INVENTORY BAG IS A BACKPACK. It had been a barbell and then a loaf, and
both times the fault was the same: a holdall is a horizontal blob, and a
horizontal blob with anything on either end is something else. There is no
version of that shape that survives being twenty pixels wide. A backpack is a
vertical object with three parts that are still three parts when it is tiny --
the body, the pocket over the bottom half of it, and the strap looping off the
side. Its top is a shallow curve rather than the half-circle it was first given,
which had a balloon sat on a squashed bag.

THE SPRAY CAN IS THE OLD ONE AGAIN. The redraw made a tidier can and a worse
icon -- the spray shrank to three dots tucked up against the cap, where they
vanish, and what was left was a plain cylinder with a band across it. A plain
cylinder is a battery, a lighter, a drinks can, anything. The thing that says
aerosol is what is coming OUT of it, and the old one threw three fat dots clear
of the nozzle where they can actually be seen.

A COMPATIBILITY PASS: THE TICK STOPS DELETING THE WORLD, AND NOTHING DEPRECATED IS LEFT.

Every native the mod calls -- five hundred and twenty-seven of them -- was
checked against ScriptHookVDotNet's own table, and all of them are in it.
The copy in the game folder is byte-identical to the one the mod is built
against. That was the biggest single risk and it is clear.

THE TICK'S OWN CATCH WAS THE WORST THING IN THE MOD. On any caught exception
it ran the full world teardown: fifty-odd systems, every scene, car and ped
the mod had placed, deleted, and then rebuilt on the next frame. One
transient null in one subsystem and the yard blinked out of existence. It
was also a long job inside a frame that had already thrown, which is the
shape of thing ScriptHookVDotNet kills scripts for. A single failed tick
does not stop the mod, so it no longer tears anything down; the teardown
runs only when the mod actually stops, on parking and on unload.

TWENTY-ONE CALLS TO DEPRECATED API ARE GONE. Game.GenerateHash, the old
Player.WantedLevel, Game.Version and Game.IsLoading are all marked obsolete
because Script Hook V is removing what they sit on, and a newer SHVDN can
break them. Each is replaced with the version-proof call the mod already
used everywhere else -- GET_HASH_KEY, Player.Wanted, Game.FileVersion, and
a check on the screen fade for the one thing IsLoading was standing in for.
Compiler warnings went from thirty-six to fifteen, and every one that is
left is an unused field, which cannot fail.

Also checked and found sound: number parsing and formatting is culture-
invariant throughout, so a German or French Windows reads the same ini and
save as an English one; and the only wait that could spin forever was the
one fixed last build -- the other two use SHVDN's own bounded wait.


THE FREEZE ON THE WAY OUT OF A ROOM.

A player sent the log that named it, which is the only reason this was
findable:

    Blocking script! Hoodrich.Main was terminated because it caused the
    game to freeze too long
      SHVDN.Script.Wait
      InteriorDoor.Wait
      InteriorDoor.Leave

The wait between fading out and fading back in was a loop on the game's
own clock and nothing else. That clock stops when the game is paused -- so
a pause during the fade, or anything else that stalls it, left the loop
spinning on a condition that could never become true. ScriptHookVDotNet
kills a script that does not come back, and killing it is the freeze.

It is bounded by the real clock now as well, which cannot stop. Worst case
is a fade half a second longer than intended instead of a dead script.

The grow room and the pill press are gone from this build anyway, so the
two doors people hit this on are not there to hit -- but every door added
through the ini went through the same wait, and now none of them can hang.

THE ATTEMPT TO MAKE TRIGGER SMALLER IS OUT, AND IT WAS WRONG TO SHIP IT.

a_c_poodle. Middling: a_c_retriever, a_c_shepherd. Big: a_c_rottweiler,

THE TAKEOVER

It is one night in three now, not every night. A thing that is always on is
scenery, and the point of this one is turning up to it. The nights are counted
rather than worked out from the date, because the day of the month wraps at the
end of it and the special case is the part that would be wrong. Hoodrich.ini
has the number under [Block] if one in three is not the rhythm you want.


One junction on Carson, one night, some time between nine and four. Cars and
people arrive from the surrounding streets, a ring of them parks facing in, and
four of them spin the back end round on four places in the junction until the blue
lights turn up. It runs
for three hours and it happens whether you go or not -- the whole value of it is
driving past at two in the morning and finding it.

Nothing appears. Every person walks in and every car drives in, from out of
sight, to a place picked for them before they existed. Eighteen places walked
kerb by kerb, screenshot by screenshot -- fourteen for the ring and four for the
performers -- and no car ends up anywhere else.

The crowd is the neighbourhood the junction is in. They film it, drink, talk,
step back when a car comes close, run when there is a gunshot, and back off when
somebody gets clipped. Nobody claps. Flares get thrown over it, somebody sets a
firework up in the road, a helicopter comes over halfway through, and the block
posts about it once when it starts and again when it has been going a while.


TAKING YOUR OWN PRODUCT

Every gram in this mod existed to be bought, cut, bagged, carried and sold, and
the one thing you could never do with it was use it. Now you can, from the
pocket screen, and each of the seven does something none of the others do:

  weed      time slows and the world drifts. Nothing is urgent.
  oxy       everything softens. Half damage, healing as you walk, no speed.
  bars      a bright shiny day, and you cannot walk in a straight line.
  crack     rage. Time drags, you hit twice as hard, forty seconds.
  coke      speed -- the fastest the game allows -- bright and sharp with it.
  meth      the long one. Five minutes wired and a minute wrecked after.
  heroin    the trip. Half speed, trails, and you are not going anywhere.

You watch yourself take it, with the right thing in your hand -- a joint or a
blunt, a pipe, a needle, a wrap, a bottle to wash pills down. The effect does not
land until the ritual finishes, and the joint carries on until you walk away from
it. The hard ones cost you a comedown afterwards.

Mix them. Three at once is a night, and past three the lights go out: you drop,
the screen fuzzes, and you come round four to nine hours later on the far side
of the map with no car. Three in you and running means your legs stop agreeing
with you, and bars roughly double the odds of going over.


THE PHONE

Rebuilt. The apps are icons on a grid rather than boxes, the one under the
cursor lights up and punches when you choose it, and the header is a
notification shade that rolls through whatever is worth telling you -- a raid on
your block, a debt due, one line per person who has texted, a takeover running.
A bank card sits along the bottom: balance, last transfer in, and everything the
corner has ever paid.

Messages are a messaging app now. Bubbles the shape of what is in them rather
than a column of identical grey slabs, and you can reply to anybody who texts
you -- matched to what they were actually talking about, and they sometimes
answer back.


TURF ON THE MAP

Whose block is whose, shaded on the minimap and the pause map. Walked corner by
corner and measured out of the game rather than guessed at, cut into strips that
sit on the street grid instead of floating discs over it. Cross into your own
block during a raid and every one of yours comes for you.

THE SAVE FOLLOWS THE SAVE, NOT THE FOLDER'S PERMISSIONS

Reported on the mod page: quit the game, load it back up, and everything the mod
had tracked is reset.

The mod writes beside the dll when Windows lets it and falls back to
Documents\Hoodrich when it does not, which is the normal case under Program
Files. That part has worked for months. What was wrong is that loading only ever
looked at whichever of the two won THIS session -- so the save location was a
function of the folder's permissions, and permissions change.

Run one cannot write to the game folder and saves to Documents. Then somebody
runs the game as administrator once, or takes ownership of the folder, or an
installer resets the ACL. Run two CAN write there, finds no save in it, and
starts them at rank zero. The playthrough is still sitting in Documents,
untouched, and nothing ever looks at it again.

Which is also why granting yourself full control appeared to fix it. That was
never the fix. It just stopped the location moving about.

So the question asked first is no longer where CAN it write. It is where IS the
save, and only when neither place holds one does writability decide. A folder
holding nothing but a .bak counts: that is still the folder with your
playthrough in it.

NOTHING WAS EVER DELETED. If this happened to you, your old save is in
Documents\Hoodrich and this build will find it and carry on from it.


AND IT SAYS SO NOW

The startup check that was meant to warn about this could never fire. It probed
the folder the mod had ALREADY fallen back to, so the probe passed every time.

It now tells you on screen when your save is not beside the dll, names the folder
it is in, and says not to go changing the game folder's permissions looking for
it.
