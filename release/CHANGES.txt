Six outfits at the closet, and three things an audit found that had never

NOBODY OF OURS SHOOTS AT THE POLICE. Everyone of the set carries now, so one
patrol rolling past a hangout was the whole night gone -- a corner man letting
off at a squad car turns a block into a siege. Anybody wearing one of the mod's
gang groups who has decided to fight an officer is taken out of it and leaves
instead, which is what people on a corner actually do.

THE HOMIES ARE THE EXCEPTION, and only half of it: the two riding with you stop
shooting at the law but do not run, because running would be leaving you in the
car. A fight with a rival is untouched either way.

GERALD IS OFF THE ECSTASY. He was texting discounts on it.

THE ASBO SAYS LOTABASS.


YOUR OWN CARS RIDE LOW. Get into one you paid Hao for and the driver's window
winds down, once, on the way in. Under about a jogging pace the arm goes out of
it, and it comes back in when you get going. Only cars that are actually yours,
and only when you are the one driving.

AN ASBO ON THE PAD AT LAMAR'S, facing the other way. Competition springs and
the biggest speakers the body owns, asked for by the shop's own words rather
than by an index, with the boot held open over them the way the one by the
decks is.

AND NOTHING GETS A WHEELIE BAR. Benny's puts one at the end of the chassis list
on several bodies and the build takes the last option in every slot, so a car
parked outside a house had a drag bar hanging off the back of it. It also fits
the speakers and the boot now -- a boot held open over an empty carpeted well
was the one thing worse than a boot shut.


THE CAR BY THE DECKS AT LAMAR'S IS A BUCCANEER CUSTOM.

It has been three things in two days and this is the one it was reaching for.
The argument for a van was that a boot opening on a load space is where music
comes from -- which held while it was a Minivan Custom pretending, and did not
survive the car actually being a lowrider. A Buccaneer with the boot up over
the pumps IS where the sound is, and it is the shape anybody means when they
say lowrider. Everything else about it is unchanged: the whole shop, the rams
sat all the way down, the set's green inside and out, the green underglow and
West Coast Classics on until two in the morning.


SIX OUTFITS AT THE CLOSET, the way the game's own wardrobe does it.

The rail at Denise's could dress him and remember what he had on, and that was
all: one look, and changing it lost the last one. There are six pegs on it now.
Hang up what he is wearing, give it a name -- or press escape and it is called
Outfit 3 -- and put it back on whenever you are at the closet. The four rows
sit at the top of the rail above the clothes, because which outfit is a bigger
question than which shoes.

Named when it is hung rather than on a row of its own, and hanging over a peg
that already has something on it offers its old name back, so that is the
rename as well. They are filed under the body they were worn on, for the same
reason what he has on always has been: a drawable number is an index into one
model's wardrobe and means something else entirely on another.

THE CREWS ON FOOT NEVER STOOD DOWN.

Every ambient system in the mod can be told to stay in while something louder
is happening -- the rollers, the lowriders, the tow truck, the taxi. Walkers
has the same switch, its comment says the house script wires it, and the house
script never did. So the one system that puts three or four men on a pavement
kept putting them there through raids, through paybacks and through a
takeover, while everything else round that corner had gone in.


LAMAR'S TAG JOB NEVER WROTE A TAG.

The letters were built: a glyph table, a layout that turns a word into points
on a wall, and a routine that picks which word. All three were correct and none
of them was ever called, so the wall always got the random splatter that is
meant to be the fallback for a tag nobody has drawn. Every tag in the game has
been the fallback since the letters went in. It writes the set's own tag now,
and still falls back when a character has no glyph.


TWO GANGS WERE DRAWN THE WRONG COLOUR ON THE MAP.

The Aztecas call themselves a dark cyan and the Marabunta a lighter one, and
both were on the blue blip -- so neither was its own colour and the two could
not be told apart. They are on the two cyans now, which is what their own data
says they are.

Two fixes off the first day of reports.

THE PHONE STOPPED WORKING AT THE SAFE HOUSE, and so did the kitchen.

Walking into Franklin's runs the game's own house scripts -- the bed, the
wardrobe, the television -- and any of them may raise the mission flag and
leave it raised while you potter about indoors. This mod stands down while
that flag is up, which is right on a story mission and wrong in your own
kitchen: from where you are standing the phone simply dies and the counter
stops answering, with nothing on screen to say why.

The flag no longer stands the mod down inside the house. Every other reason
still does -- control off, a cutscene, a faded screen, the pause menu -- and
those are the ones that are actually true when a mission is running.

If you are on 0.6.0 and cannot update, PauseDuringMission=false in the ini
works around it.


===============================================================================
  NEW
===============================================================================


THE FEED CAN BE TURNED OFF ON SCREEN, which it could not be before.

TweetsOnTheRight only ever chose which side of the screen a post landed on.
This is the setting that means neither: nothing is drawn, nothing goes into the
game's own notification stack, and so nothing chimes either. The timeline still
fills, so it is all there to read in the phone whenever you want it -- it just
stops narrating itself at you while you play.

Three ways to work it. FeedOnScreen under [Socials] in Hoodrich.ini, a tick on
the settings screen, and a key -- NumPad * by default -- that toggles it where
you stand and writes the answer back to the ini so it survives a restart.
FeedKey=None under [Socials] takes the key away for anybody who wants it back.

  0.6.0  (6 September 2026)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three hundred and fifty-eight changes since 0.5.1, and more of them than usual
are things the mod could not do at all before.


===============================================================================
  NEW
===============================================================================


THE TAKEOVER

It is one night in three now, not every night. A thing that is always on is
scenery, and the point of this one is turning up to it. The nights are counted
rather than worked out from the date, because the day of the month wraps at the
end of it and the special case is the part that would be wrong. Hoodrich.ini
has the number under [Block] if one in three is not the rhythm you want.


One junction on Carson, one night, some time between nine and four. Cars and
people arrive from the surrounding streets, a ring of them parks facing in, and
four of them spin the back end round on four places in the junction until the blue
lights turn up. It runs
for three hours and it happens whether you go or not -- the whole value of it is
driving past at two in the morning and finding it.

Nothing appears. Every person walks in and every car drives in, from out of
sight, to a place picked for them before they existed. Eighteen places walked
kerb by kerb, screenshot by screenshot -- fourteen for the ring and four for the
performers -- and no car ends up anywhere else.

The crowd is the neighbourhood the junction is in. They film it, drink, talk,
step back when a car comes close, run when there is a gunshot, and back off when
somebody gets clipped. Nobody claps. Flares get thrown over it, somebody sets a
firework up in the road, a helicopter comes over halfway through, and the block
posts about it once when it starts and again when it has been going a while.


TAKING YOUR OWN PRODUCT

Every gram in this mod existed to be bought, cut, bagged, carried and sold, and
the one thing you could never do with it was use it. Now you can, from the
pocket screen, and each of the seven does something none of the others do:

  weed      time slows and the world drifts. Nothing is urgent.
  oxy       everything softens. Half damage, healing as you walk, no speed.
  bars      a bright shiny day, and you cannot walk in a straight line.
  crack     rage. Time drags, you hit twice as hard, forty seconds.
  coke      speed -- the fastest the game allows -- bright and sharp with it.
  meth      the long one. Five minutes wired and a minute wrecked after.
  heroin    the trip. Half speed, trails, and you are not going anywhere.

You watch yourself take it, with the right thing in your hand -- a joint or a
blunt, a pipe, a needle, a wrap, a bottle to wash pills down. The effect does not
land until the ritual finishes, and the joint carries on until you walk away from
it. The hard ones cost you a comedown afterwards.

Mix them. Three at once is a night, and past three the lights go out: you drop,
the screen fuzzes, and you come round four to nine hours later on the far side
of the map with no car. Three in you and running means your legs stop agreeing
with you, and bars roughly double the odds of going over.


THE PHONE

Rebuilt. The apps are icons on a grid rather than boxes, the one under the
cursor lights up and punches when you choose it, and the header is a
notification shade that rolls through whatever is worth telling you -- a raid on
your block, a debt due, one line per person who has texted, a takeover running.
A bank card sits along the bottom: balance, last transfer in, and everything the
corner has ever paid.

Messages are a messaging app now. Bubbles the shape of what is in them rather
than a column of identical grey slabs, and you can reply to anybody who texts
you -- matched to what they were actually talking about, and they sometimes
answer back.


TURF ON THE MAP

Whose block is whose, shaded on the minimap and the pause map. Walked corner by
corner and measured out of the game rather than guessed at, cut into strips that
sit on the street grid instead of floating discs over it. Cross into your own
block during a raid and every one of yours comes for you.


THE MUFFLER SHOP

A low-end Los Santos Customs on the corner of Hao's yard, with a spanner on the
map. Drive up to the roller door and it asks; say yes and the car is driven in
for you while a camera watches from the side, the screen goes down, and it
comes back up on the car in the bay with the menu over it. Respray, second
colour, pearlescent, wheels and wheel colour, engine, brakes, transmission,
suspension, armour, turbo, every body part the car has, horn, tint, xenons,
tyre smoke, neon, livery and plate style -- each by the game's own name, each
going onto the car as the cursor lands on it, at under half the price of the
shop over the river. Drive out and, if the car is one of yours, the whole kit is
read off it and kept: it is stood up with its rims on from then on.


THE MASK

An app that puts a balaclava on and takes it off -- and the balaclava means
something. The police are looking for whoever they last had eyes on, a man in
a mask or a bare face. Change that while they are not looking, round a corner
with the stars grey, and the man they are after stops existing: they ignore
you and the search runs out on its own. Change it in front of them and they
have simply watched you do it. Put it back on while they are ignoring you and
that is the face they want again. Nothing touches the wanted level itself.


KNOWAI, PROPERLY

You pick the place before the car is sent. The app opens a picker with the
place itself behind it, live -- a camera goes out to it and circles it while
you look -- with the fare from where you are stood and about how long it will
take. Twenty-five places, from Aunt Denise's to Paleto Bay. The car comes and
pulls away for the place the moment you are in. Drop a marker on the map
mid-ride and it becomes a stop on the way: the car swings by, waits, and
carries on. The tile on the phone says what it is.


PLATES

Buy a car off Hao and a small panel comes up over it: what the plate says, on
the game's own keyboard, and which of the thirteen styles it is -- the story's
six and the seven the tuners added -- each going onto the car as you land on
it. The plate is how the mod finds the car again, so two of yours cannot wear
the same one, and Hao knows a car of yours by the plate it actually wears.


STRETCH'S COUNTER

Redrawn, and the pictures work for the first time. The stock down the left by
rack; the gun you are on laid out on the right, its picture large, what it is
for, rounds by the box with what you are holding, and a shelf of everything
that bolts to it -- scopes, sights, suppressors, muzzles, grips, lights,
barrels, magazines and special rounds, whatever this gun actually takes. R
steps across to the shelf. Enter fits a part and takes the money; Enter on a
fitted one takes it off, and putting it back is free. Fitted parts survive a
save and come back with the gun.


THE POCKET, THE STASH HOUSE AND THE KITCHEN, REDRAWN

Three screens that show the same product, and they had each grown their own
letterhead, their own capacity bar and their own idea of a key hint -- one
wrote the keys as a run-on sentence, one as icons, one as both. They are built
out of one set of parts now and agree with each other.

The letterhead is one row where it was three: the wordmark, then the room in
capitals with what it is in small words beside it and the count on the right,
then a rule. A sixth of the screen of introduction is gone from each.

Capacity is a meter: the figure on the right, a track under it with a bright
tip on the fill, green while there is room, amber as it fills, red when it is
full, easing toward the truth rather than jumping to it. The stash house has
one for each side.

The keys are drawn as keys: a small cap with the letter on it and the errand
in dim words after, the arrows as arrows, and the way out in the same corner on
every screen.

THE POCKET has a card under the tiles that says what the chosen one is: the
picture large, the name in full, a tag for bagged or raw weight, the amount
rolling as you put it down, how stepped on it is in words, and the percentage
with its mark on the right. It slides in as the plate comes up under the tile.

THE STASH HOUSE reads as two places. The name has the left half of the line;
the right half is two columns on their own grounds, ON YOU and AT HOME, with a
gap between them that you operate. Press a direction and the figure counts down
on one side and up on the other while chevrons run across the gap the way it
went. Raw weight says so on a tag rather than as a longer name.

THE KITCHEN has a gauge for how far you step on it: the four cuts on one row,
each with the mark it leaves on the bag, the lit plate sliding to the one you
are on, the cuts stronger than what is on the counter greyed and locked, the
one under the floor in red. What is on the counter is written on the right of
the gauge so the locked ones explain themselves. The batch is a card: what goes
in on the left, chevrons running through the press, what comes out, and what
it is worth large on the right -- green while it can start, red when there is
no room for what would come out.

Nothing about what any of the three DO has changed. Every key does what it did.


THE FLARES FLY AGAIN. A throw handed to somebody stood in a scenario is a
throw the game quietly drops, and once the crowd started cheering that was most
of them. A thrower is taken out of whatever they were doing first now.

THE CROWD CHEERS AND FILMS

While a car is sideways in the circle the crowd reacts to it: one or two more
of them every second or so turn to the middle and either cheer or hold the
phone up to film, for a few seconds at a time, never more than two thirds of
them at once. When the cars stop the last of them wind down within a few
seconds and go back to standing about.

EVERYTHING IN A SCENE IS SOLID, OR SAYS WHY NOT. A prop is stood up the moment
its scene comes into range, up to two hundred metres out, and one made before
the game has streamed anything round it can come up with no body at all --
a picture of a fridge you walk through. Every prop is looked at once the
ground round it has loaded, and one without a body is given one. A model that
has no collision to give is named in the log. A scene's own placements are no
longer taken for duplicates of each other, so both lines on the bar get laid.

THE LOT BEHIND INNOCENCE HAS PEOPLE ON IT. One of the set leaning on the wall
by the FR36, one on a joint, one sat on the steps with a beer -- the seated
drinking animation played at a point on the lower step, so he sits on the
landing with his feet on the steps and no chair -- a box on the corner with
the music up,
and one round the corner tagging the wall, on the game's own spraying loop
with a can in his left hand -- the hand the clip sprays with -- and black
paint coming out of it, in bursts, the way a real can is worked. The FR36 is
plain metallic green, no pearl.

A CAR OF THE SET'S ON THE B/HOLES LOT ON INNOCENCE, green on Benny's rims,
engine running with West Coast Classics up loud, one of the set on a
cigarette by the wall next to it and one on his phone by the car.

THE SHOPS IN STRAWBERRY have two of the set outside, one on a beer and one on
a cigarette, a car of the set's with the stereo up, and a couple sat on
whatever there is to sit on -- the game is asked for the nearest seat to each
of them -- talking.

THE BIKE MEET AT LEROY'S, two nights in five: three bikes in a fan on the lot,
two with their riders still sat on them, three more of the set stood in front.

THE CORNERS TALK. Every hangout -- the meets, the lot, the Checkout, the
B/Holes lot, Strawberry, Leroy's -- posts once a night when its people come
out: the set saying they are posted up, come through, bring your own; or a
neighbour saying they can hear the bass from their kitchen.

THE ONES WALKING THE BLOCK HOLD THEIR BEER RIGHT. It is the game's own bottle
now, on the right hand's prop bone -- the hand the drinking clip drinks with --
where it was a scene bottle in the left, at an angle nobody has ever held a
beer at. Some of them carry a cigarette instead. A crew on its way somewhere
stops now and again: a drink for the ones with a beer, a drag for the ones
with a smoke, the rest stood about with them, and then on the way they were
going. And they talk to each other rather than at the street: one says
something and turns to another, and that one answers him a couple of seconds
later, loud enough to hear from the end of the road. The phones and bottles on
the corners moved to that hand with them.

THE CROWD BUYS FROM YOU. Post up and serve at a takeover or at a party and the
people already there are customers like anybody else -- they always passed the
corner's own test, and then the thing holding them to their spot walked them
straight back before they could reach you, every time. Whoever is mid-deal is
left alone, and when it is done the same pass that was dragging him back walks
him to his mark and starts him on what he was doing.

THREE CARS ARE ALWAYS AT IT. The field was entirely a dice roll, and a field
with no faces in it is one nobody recognises: the Emperor Vectre in the set's
green, an FR36 and a Gauntlet Hellfire turn up to every takeover, and
everything else arrives to a scene that already has somebody in it.

EVERY CAR THAT COMPETES IS ON COMPETITION SPRINGS, asked for by the shop's own
name for the part rather than by an index -- index three is Competition on some
cars, Race on others and nothing at all on the ones with a shorter list. And
none of them wears a livery, cleared after the paint and after every body part
is on, which is where a model that keeps liveries in a mod slot hands one back.

THE BURNOUTS DO NOT PAUSE. Each burst of lock was asked for again four hundred
milliseconds before the last one ran out, and the takeover only thinks every
seven hundred -- so the top-up arrived late, every burst, and every car doing
donuts stopped dead for a third of a second every three seconds all night. The
lead is longer than a tick now.

AND THEY ONLY GATHER UP IN THE PEOPLE. A performer was sent back to its mark
the moment it had drifted a few metres, so the show was mostly cars braking and
re-driving their own marks. A donut that travels is a donut: it keeps going for
as long as there is empty road under it, and it slows, stops and drives back
when it has reached the crowd. The distance rule is only a backstop now, for a
car that has slid clean off the junction where there is nobody to be near.

THE SHOW HAPPENS ON THE MARKS. A performer was called arrived eight metres from
its walked mark and leashed at eleven, which is a car going round near the
junction rather than on the place walked for it. Five and eight now, and it
gets forty seconds to drive there instead of twenty-five. Nothing is placed:
it drives the whole way and is left where it stops, as before.

SPECTATORS PARK ON A WALKED KERB OR THEY GO HOME. The parking task was allowed
to run out and whatever it had managed became the space -- a lane, a junction,
the wrong side of the road -- and the log said so every time and left it there.
A car that finishes somewhere else is offered another free kerb and leaves if
there is not one. Nothing is moved to make it work.

NOTHING AT A TAKEOVER WEARS A LIVERY. It was being taken off before the
bodywork went on, and on the models that keep a livery in a mod slot rather
than the old one, fitting a body part hands it back. It comes off last now.

THE POLICE RADIO IS QUIETER. The call that goes out when an officer decides to
come and look at you was playing at the volume of a man stood in front of you
talking, and it is a walkie-talkie in somebody else's hand thirty metres away.
Four tenths of the voice volume now, under [Voice] in Hoodrich.ini and on the
settings screen.

A BUTTON PER JUNCTION on the settings screen, in place of the one that only
ever meant Carson. Each starts a takeover where it says and says so, and the
one you start by hand stays where you started it.

THE TAKEOVER CAN HAPPEN AT MORE THAN ONE JUNCTION. Every mark it used -- the
middle, the circle, the corners, the stages, the kerbs -- was a constant, which
was right while there was one crossroads and is the whole file the moment there
are two. They belong to a junction now and the names in the code did not
change. The second one is on Davis: two performers rather than four, five
corners and fourteen kerbs, all walked. They take turns rather than being
rolled for -- one takeover on Carson, the next on Davis, and back -- because a
coin that comes up the same three times running is a takeover that reads as
being in one place. A junction not walked all the way is never used.

THE SOUND VAN IS A MOONBEAM CUSTOM. The Minivan Custom before it was a Benny's
body but it was still a people carrier with a kit on it, and dropping that only
made a low people carrier. The Moonbeam is the lowrider van, and it keeps the
whole reason a van is parked by the decks rather than a saloon.

THE SOUND VAN IS A LOWRIDER AND ITS BOOT STAYS UP. The whole shop on it now,
inside and out, and the rams fitted and sat all the way down -- fitting a set
of hydraulics does not drop a car, it takes ride height away from the
suspension slot and hands it to the rams, which come out of the shop halfway
up. And
the boot is actually held open: the code that holds a door was written, was
commented, and was never called once -- so the van opened its boot on the frame
it was made and the game shut it a second later, every time.

TAO SAYS GOODBYE OUT LOUD, AND HIS CAPTIONS ARE GONE. Every way out of a
dealer's screen -- an option that ends it, or backing out -- plays his
recorded farewell, every time. The yellow captions over a delivery are gone:
a delivery line plays its recording if there is one and is otherwise quiet,
and is logged, named, for the to-record list.

FRANKLIN'S BIKE IS A SANCHEZ. The Bagger the game parks outside his house is
swapped, as it appears, for a Sanchez on its first livery -- same plate, same
paint -- and he is put back on it if he was sat on it. The game puts the
Bagger back now and then and it is swapped again. Hoodrich.ini, [Cars],
FranklinsBike names another bike or leaves the Bagger; FranklinsBikeLivery
picks the livery.

NO RAID IN THE FIRST TEN MINUTES. The roll for an unprovoked raid started the
moment the mod loaded, and with the Vagos already at beef that was one game
start in four opening on Stretch shouting that they were outside. Nothing
comes for the block until ten minutes after loading now. A war you start
yourself is not held back.

THE FAMILIES NEVER TURN ON FRANKLIN. His own set cannot cross into beef with
him however low their number goes, their leaders take a knock from him without
drawing on him, naming them on the feed does not send a car round, and his own
people's greetings and street talk are not swallowed any more by the block the
mod puts on the game's wrong-neighbourhood lines.

STRETCH'S COUNTER SHOWS ITS PISTOLS AGAIN. A pack that had not streamed in yet
was being counted as a pack that did not have the gun, and with the search
already done that was written down as a permanent no -- which is how every
pistol lost its picture. A not-yet is a not-yet now, and a no on disk is asked
again each session. A fourth pack the game ships, mpweaponsunusedfornow, is
looked in now as well. The online guns have no photograph anywhere in the
game's files -- every archive on the install was searched -- so they keep
their names.

GERALD'S TRUCK ON THE STREET faces north now, the way that side of the road
goes.

THE TWO WITH THE MECHANIC AT HAO'S are there six days in ten; the mechanic is
always there. A scene file can say "sometimes 60: <handles>" in its Note.

THE CORNERS ARE NOT THERE EVERY DAY. The lot behind Innocence, the Checkout,
the B/Holes lot and the shops in Strawberry each roll their own day -- six in
ten -- people, cars and box together, so some days one is empty and another
is not.

EVERYONE OF THE SET CARRIES. The ones stood about without a gun in their
hands -- on a wall, on a beer, on a phone, at a meet -- have a pistol or a
machine pistol holstered now, drawn when something starts, and none of them
runs from a fight in front of them. Their poses are exactly as they were.

AN EMPEROR OUTSIDE THE CHECKOUT ON INNOCENCE. The set's green on Benny's rims,
competition suspension, limo glass, no neon -- and two of the set by it, one
leaning on the wall and one on a beer.

AN FR36 ON THE LOT BEHIND INNOCENCE. One of the set's: the green, competition
suspension, dark smoke on the glass, green neon under it, the street kit all round -- the bumpers
and skirts picked by name, so it is the Street kit on this car and not
whatever happens to be last in the list.

A LOOKOUT AT GERALD'S WINDOW. One of the set upstairs at the window with the
binoculars, armed like the rest of the yard, so he answers an attack the way
they do.

THE MEETS ARE NOT EVERY NIGHT. A meet that is there every single night is
furniture. The meet on the court rolls a little over half its nights now, and
there is a second one at the wash on Innocence -- three cars in the lane, the
set stood round them, Radio Los Santos up -- that rolls a little under half.
Each on its own roll, so some nights it is one, some the other, some both and
some neither.

THE LEADERS CARRY A PISTOL. Gerald and the rest were already immortal and
already walked back to their spot; now each has a pistol on him he does not
show, and somebody who puts hands or a round on him gets it drawn on them --
you included -- for a quarter of a minute, after which it goes away and he
walks back to his spot as if nothing had happened.

GERALD'S DELIVERY PARKS FACING THE WAY IT CAME. The heading it was straightened
onto at the house was a hundred and eighty degrees off the way it drives in
and out. The hobo seat at the foot of his stairs faces the street as well,
rather than the wall.

A STRANGER'S CAR GETS IT AFTER THE DRIVER. Once the driver has been dealt
with -- or the crowd's temper on him has run its time -- whoever was on him
turns on the car, if it is still there: fifteen seconds of kicks and punches
from the sides and one of them up on the roof stomping, with the dents to
show for it, the odd window through, and the alarm going. A car the crowd
has been on is never removed; it stays where it ended up, as part of the
night.

THE KERB CARS PARK BY DRIVING. A spectator's car arriving at its walked spot was
set down on it by hand -- the right place and the right heading, and the one
thing at the takeover that read as broken. Near enough, the driver gets the
game's own parking task for the spot and the heading now, nose in, and the
car is parked when it has stopped on or near the spot -- or wherever it is
when the task has had its time. Nothing is moved by hand any more, not even
the ones that gave up.

THE RIDERS SIT IN WITH THE CROWD. On the line, in a gap in the ring, facing the
middle -- which they arrive facing because they come in on two legs, out to a
point on their bearing and then a straight run inward, rather than being
turned on the spot. The reasons a rider was not sent are in the log.

THE NOTIFICATIONS STOP FLASHING. The game's feed has a blink flag on a posted
item, and it was on for everything the mod called important -- ninety-odd of
them -- so a notification arrived flashing black and white for a second.
Nothing blinks now; important is said with the words and the colour. And a

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  0.5.1
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Out on its own the day after 0.5.0, because the first thing in it is save loss.

If you are on 0.4.0 or 0.5.0 with the game installed under Program Files, this
affects you, and it can take a whole playthrough without ever saying anything.


THE SAVE FOLLOWS THE SAVE, NOT THE FOLDER'S PERMISSIONS

Reported on the mod page: quit the game, load it back up, and everything the mod
had tracked is reset.

The mod writes beside the dll when Windows lets it and falls back to
Documents\Hoodrich when it does not, which is the normal case under Program
Files. That part has worked for months. What was wrong is that loading only ever
looked at whichever of the two won THIS session -- so the save location was a
function of the folder's permissions, and permissions change.

Run one cannot write to the game folder and saves to Documents. Then somebody
runs the game as administrator once, or takes ownership of the folder, or an
installer resets the ACL. Run two CAN write there, finds no save in it, and
starts them at rank zero. The playthrough is still sitting in Documents,
untouched, and nothing ever looks at it again.

Which is also why granting yourself full control appeared to fix it. That was
never the fix. It just stopped the location moving about.

So the question asked first is no longer where CAN it write. It is where IS the
save, and only when neither place holds one does writability decide. A folder
holding nothing but a .bak counts: that is still the folder with your
playthrough in it.

NOTHING WAS EVER DELETED. If this happened to you, your old save is in
Documents\Hoodrich and this build will find it and carry on from it.


AND IT SAYS SO NOW

The startup check that was meant to warn about this could never fire. It probed
the folder the mod had ALREADY fallen back to, so the probe passed every time.

It now tells you on screen when your save is not beside the dll, names the folder
it is in, and says not to go changing the game folder's permissions looking for
it.


AND TWO SMALLER ONES

  0.5.0

They talk now.


A HUNDRED AND FIFTY-TWO RECORDED LINES

Gerald, Lamar, Tao Cheng, Hao and Stretch say what the screen says. The whole
tutorial, both of Gerald's packages, the port run end to end, Lamar's six jobs
and the barks on the ride out, Hao's yard, the shop when a plug drives to your
door -- spoken.

It degrades cleanly and it is optional. A line with no recording is text, which
is what every line was until now, so nothing depends on it. [Voice] in
Hoodrich.ini turns it off, sets the level or makes it repeat.

A performance is spent once. Hearing a man explain the business a fourth time is
a speech being shouted at somebody re-reading a menu, so a line you have heard is
remembered per save and the screen simply shows it. Mission barks are the
exception -- they belong to a job you can run again, and they play every time.

And the speaker's mouth moves while he is talking.


THE PHONE RINGS

Lamar calls fifteen seconds after you sign on with the Families. The handset
comes out with his name and his face on it, no apps, and two buttons. It rings
with your own character's ringtone, and it does not slow time or blur the world:
a phone going off is not a menu, and the street should carry on around it.

Let it ring out and he texts instead. He is not a man who stands there redialling.


NOISES THAT MEAN SOMETHING

A text arriving chimes. A patrol taking an interest in you while you are posted
up puts a dispatch call out over the radio -- before the officer starts walking,
which is the point of it.


THE SPRAY CAN IS ON THE PHONE

Overspray's paint engine, as an app. Free-hand spraying with the real thing:
thirteen colours including chrome and gold, three cap sizes that change how fine
the line goes, strokes drawn as stretched decals rather than chains of dots, and
paint that runs if you hold it on one spot.

Lamar's tag jobs use it. You walk in, the can appears in his hand, and you spray
the wall yourself.

Marks survive a reload, and the decal budget is the real limit -- the app says so
and names the file that raises it.


WEIGHT IS SOLD THE WAY WEIGHT IS SOLD

Every row on a buy screen used to cost the same money for a different amount,
because a lot was a budget divided by a price. Nobody is offered a hundred
thousand dollars' worth of anything; they are offered a kilo, and a kilo of one
thing costs more than a kilo of another.

The unit belongs to the product now. Fifty kilos of weed and five of cocaine are
both what a container moves -- the cheap bulky thing has to travel in quantity to
be worth the trip. A dealer takes his own fraction of it, so the same catalogue
reads as kilos off the port, a quarter of that on a corner, and grams off a man
on a pushbike.

Purity followed it. It is measured in his own lots rather than in money: he
breaks up what he sells small and leaves weight alone.


HAO BUYS THEM BACK

Sell a car back to him from the conversation rather than only from the driver's
seat. Half of what you paid, or all of it inside ten minutes -- not because he is
soft, because it is not in the book yet. It goes back on its spot, locked and up
for sale.


THE LAW HAS OPINIONS ABOUT PAINT

An officer who SEES you tagging arrests you rather than shooting at you. One
star, held at one for as long as a can is the worst thing in your hands. Pull an
actual gun and that stops applying.

It wants a clear line of sight and an officer roughly facing you. One the other
side of the wall you are painting has not seen anything.


FIXES

Your guns come back with what was bolted to them. The locker returned the weapon
and dropped the scope, the grip, the suppressor and the light every time.

The homies follow you the moment you get on the bike. They were told to follow
while you were still walking to yours, took the no-vehicle branch, and rode off
on their own.

The first press of "let them know how you feel" works. The animation was asked
for and played in the same breath, so the first one always went to a clip that
had not loaded and only the second landed.

Resetting the mod clears your messages. The feed and the inbox are separate
stores and only one of them was named, so a man wiped back to nobody still had
Gerald texting him about work he had never done.

The bay marker stops drawing once you are stood in it, instead of painting a
green disc over the truck you are reversing.

Tao walks to your door straight. The drunk stagger was his and it is gone.

A leader introduces himself once. After that he looks up and says what's up,
rather than greeting you identically forever.

  0.4.0
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The city has an opinion now, and the mod tells you when it cannot start.

Read the FIXES section first if you have been running 0.3.0 -- one of them could
have cost you a save and it was in every build until this one.


WHERE YOU STAND MATTERS

Every gram sold on a block makes the next customer there slower to turn up, and
it recovers while you are somewhere else. It moves how OFTEN somebody walks up,
never what they pay -- a worked-out corner goes quiet, it does not haggle. An
ounce barely registers; a kilo dries a block for an hour. The phone says how
worked over the block is under Gangs > This block.

And word gets round that somewhere is busy, which it genuinely is. That is the
first time anything on the feed has been worth acting on rather than worth having
caused. It never names the block you are already standing on.


THE HOUSE CAN BE TAKEN FROM YOU

Nothing had any claim on what was in Denise's, which made it a bank and quietly
made the dead drop pointless. Get loud enough, hold enough, and somebody comes
while you are out -- police if it is heat that brought them, whoever you are
beefing with if it is not. They take a share.

You are texted three minutes before anybody arrives. Move it to the drop, carry
it, sell it, or take the hit. Being at the house when they come calls it off
outright -- the message said come home and you came home. Under 250g nobody is
interested, so the threat arrives with the hoard.


ELEVEN MORE PLUGS, AND A REASON TO PICK BETWEEN THEM

Supply was Gerald and the port, and four of the seven products had no street
source at all. Now: eight gang corners selling only what their set moves, and
three independents where no set holds -- meth past the yards after dark, coke on
the west side at night, crack and brown in La Mesa around the clock.

You have to FIND them. A dealer is a man on a corner until you have stood in
front of him; after that he is a number, and he says so in his own words. His
blip does not exist until you are within about thirteen metres, and once you know
him his spot is marked permanently with the crown.

The corner is cheap because what he hands you has already been halved -- fine,
until you want to cut it yourself. Buy weight in one go and it comes uncut. Tao
is the high roller and does not get out of bed under a hundred grand; everybody
else does five, ten and twenty. Some nights the same man has something nobody has
touched, and he wants paying for it.

Each one has his own delivery car in his set's colours, his own lines for pulling
up, carrying it in, putting it down and leaving, and his own texts.

And plugs ring you out of the blue -- a real discount on one man, with a clock on
it, in Messages. Only ever from somebody you have met.


IT TELLS YOU WHEN IT IS BROKEN

If anything is wrong that the mod can see, you get a red panel naming the fault
and the fix: a ScriptHookVDotNet that does not recognise your game (nearly always
the answer on Enhanced), a data folder that did not get copied, a data folder
from a different version than the dll, a file that is present but not valid JSON,
a dealer pointing at a gang that no longer exists, an install Windows will not
let it write to. A crash names what it was loading rather than an exception type.

If you get NOTHING at all, the mod never loaded and no code of ours could have
told you. Read TROUBLESHOOTING.txt in this zip -- it leads with the Enhanced trap,
which is that Legacy and Enhanced need different ScriptHookV builds, the files
have the same name, and copying a working Legacy setup across gets you silence.


FIXES

* A SAVE THAT COULD NOT BE READ WAS TREATED AS NO SAVE AT ALL. A locked file or
  one truncated write, and the mod logged "starting fresh at rank 0" and then
  autosaved a blank state over your playthrough. It now knows the difference,
  falls back to the backup it had been writing and never reading, and if that
  fails too it switches saving off and says so rather than starting you over.

* Backing out of any screen threw a punch. The list of controls to hold was typed
  out by hand eleven times and nine copies were missing the one that swings.

* Guns bought off Stretch are yours after a reload. They still go if you are
  searched or killed.

* Tao teleported into his car; so did rollers and homies. A timeout on that task
  does not mean "give up", it means "be in that seat by then".

* Tao introduced himself on repeat port runs to a man he had handed a brick to.

* The port run carries 20 kilos. It was 250 grams, which is a jacket pocket.

* Lamar rides at your shoulder, follows for the whole job rather than only the
  ride out, hands you a pistol before the store, and talks on the way.

* The feed knows who is attacking. It announced purple on the pavement after a
  fight with the Vagos.

* Repeat missions pay a fraction of the first run. The tag list refills instead
  of running dry for good. "Spin Jamestown" is called "Spin Rancho", which is
  where it has always been.

* The taxi waits six seconds at the drop instead of driving off through the men
  getting out of it.


UPGRADING

Drop it over the top. Old saves load. Copy scripts\Hoodrich\ as well as the
dll -- keeping an old data folder is the most common way to get a mod that half
works, and this build now says so on screen when it spots it.

Hoodrich.ini is never overwritten and every new setting has a working default.
For 0.3.0 pressure with the fixes: BlockSaturationEnabled=false,
StashRaidsEnabled=false, ColdCallsEnabled=false, BlockTipsEnabled=false.
