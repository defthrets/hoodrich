0.9.8 -- 19 September 2026


ONE BAG, ONE SHELF. With Bare Minimum installed the bag had two shelves for
its food: ours, in the satchel, and theirs, in their knapsack since their
0.8.2. Food moved across on the phone went on ours; food looted or bought
went on theirs; the bag said "twelve things" on one screen and "empty" on the
other, and which was true depended on which screen you had opened last.
Theirs is the shelf now wherever their build has one, ours is the shelf
without them, and anything on the old shelf is handed across the first time
the two meet, nothing deleted. The bag's product is on the bridge as well, so
their bag screen (Bare Minimum 0.8.3 and up) shows what is in your pockets
and what is in the bag on both sides, moves it between them and takes one
from either. And a kind of product takes a place in the pockets the way a
packet of crisps does -- their pocket is five places and always counted ours,
so it read seven of five in amber. A new kind now asks for a place and goes
to the bag, or is refused, when there is none. More of a kind already on you
takes no place.


LOOTING A BODY. Every take is in the log now -- what, how much, off whom and
where it went -- because "the drugs I took off him aren't in my pocket"
arrived with no way to tell a refused take from one that landed somewhere you
were not looking. Product the pockets cannot hold goes into the bag on your
back when there is room, instead of "no room on you" beside fifteen empty
slots. And with Five0 Patrol 0.2.1 a body can be carried off the loot card:
it is brought back to life to be carried, so this mod no longer hands a man a
fresh set of pockets the moment he is set down.


A KICKABOUT ON THE BLOCK. Three of ours stood in a ring on one of five spots
round the blocks, a ball passed between them -- fast off the foot, slowing as
it arrives, turning over as it rolls, the game's own kick on whoever has it.
They watch the ball, look at whoever they kick it to, and hold it and look at
you if you stand in the ring. One spot is live at a time, picked off the
clock every three hours, and anything that hurts one of them or drags one
into a fight breaks the game up. [Block] KickaboutEnabled in the ini, a
settings row, and the block posts about it once per spot.


VERNON. "OG Vee disappeared when the mission was finished": he was still in
the passenger seat when the hand-in opened, a walk handed to a seated man
does nothing, and he sat there behind smoked glass in a car that had just
locked itself. A walk starts with getting out now, and what he says follows
what has happened -- back after the yard went wrong he says he was there,
downstairs he decides whether to go back, stood in his own basement he shows
you the shelf. The package lands somewhere a man can stand and says where,
and a man stood on the blip for six seconds with nothing in hand has it
brought to his feet. And the Dorado he delivers in sits the way the one
outside the shop does -- down at the back, nose up -- rather than arriving at
factory height. The three numbers are on his entry in dealers.json, and both
cars read them.


HAO'S LOT. Sell a car back wearing the plate you put on it and the lot no
longer recognised its own car: it left it standing when you walked off and
put a fresh one down on top of it when you came back, which is a second
Hellfire on its roof behind the first. A buy-back wears the lot's plate
again, a car of the right make locked to everybody in its own space is taken
back as the row's car whatever plate it wears, and nothing on the lot -- a
row being stocked, a car sold back, one of yours stood back up -- is put down
on a space with a car already in it. Buying refuses when the car is not
standing there, instead of taking the money for nothing.


THE CAR MEET. Nobody lies under a car: the scenario on the nose list was the
mechanic on his back with his arms up under the sill, not a man leaning into
an engine bay. The cars brake for people and for each other on the way in,
drop to car-park pace further out, and only hand the last twelve metres to
the game's blind parking manoeuvre rather than the last twenty-six with the
crowd in them.


THE TAKEOVER. Flares get thrown into the middle now, and more of them. The
flare was put in the hand and the throw asked for on the same frame, which is
a throw the game drops without a word, and the log counted it as thrown
anyway; the hand gets the flare a beat before the throw, two or three throw
every eight to sixteen seconds, nine in ten low across the cars, and the log
says how many actually left the hand.


THE FEED IN THE PHONE. A new option, off by default: [Socials] FeedOnPhone,
or "Feed in the phone" on the settings screen. Posts are then not drawn down
the right and not put into the game's notifications; they turn up on the line
at the top of the phone's home screen, with the poster's face, only while the
phone is up. Open the phone and the block is talking; put it away and it is
quiet.


THE BIKE SWAP IS GONE. Franklin keeps the game's Bagger. The swap had to run
every time he came home because the game re-supplies its bike, which was one
more thing happening on his own drive than the drive needed. FranklinsBike
and FranklinsBikeLivery are no longer read. The car keeps its swap: the
Buffalo S still becomes the STX, and FranklinsCar= left blank leaves the
game's own.


THE BOOT TAKES GUNS AGAIN. Nothing ever went in. weapons.json names a gun by
its bare id and the game speaks in WEAPON_ names, and the boot hashed the
bare one -- so the game said you were not holding the gun you were holding
and the log said "refused" on every one. The real hash is used both ways now,
in and out.


SMALLER. A fixture waits its turn per model, so the party's speaker is no
longer a ferris wheel on the card table at Lamar's. The phone's home screen
wears the drawn icons again, and Inventory wears the backpack. The bag lies
on the ground it is on rather than dead flat over a sloping pavement. And two
of Vernon's lines that were never on the record list are on it.
