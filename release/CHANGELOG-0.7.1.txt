0.7.1 -- 11 September 2026


ACID. One tab is colour -- the screen swells and the palette goes. Two tabs
doubles it. Three and the floor goes: Franklin drifts down out of the sky over
Los Santos with Radio Mirror Park playing, no parachute, slower the nearer the
ground gets, and the music fades out as he lands.

A SHOOTING RANGE WHEREVER YOU STAND THREE TARGETS UP. No coordinates. Put three
or more target props down anywhere and the mod treats them as a range: timed
trials, hits read off your own rounds, and your bests kept in the save.

A CAR MEET ON CARSON AVE. Every few nights, or from the settings screen. Twelve
of the set's cars are spawned blocks away and drive in on their own, park in
the surveyed bays, pop the bonnets, and a lowrider on juice works the switches
at the end. Everybody gets out, mingles, then breaks into threes round the
cars, and a crowd that came on foot goes bonnet to bonnet with cameras out.
Nobody is ever teleported and no two cars are the same model. Ten minutes, then
they go home.

OG VEE. Vernon leans on the wall outside Leroy's Electrical on Strawberry --
his dad's old shop, a coke operation in the basement, and a wannabe rapper's
conviction that his bars are good. He already knows you. He spits. He asks if
you're busy. The door beside him stays shut until his job is done. Fully
voiced, twenty takes, with the verses in a second voice under his other name.

HAO SELLS PILLS AND TABS. Ecstasy and acid, small and uncut, by text only --
he drives them over in his own orange Penumbra, plate HAOFAST, neons to match.
In person he sells cars, which is what he always did; the second Hao that used
to stand on his forecourt offering a drug menu is gone.

EVERY DEALER SAYS HIS OWN WORDS. The counter line and the four stash-room lines
are per dealer now, so Tao opens with "Ai-ya" and Gerald with "boy", and each
man's recording matches his own screen. Gerald's five and Tao's are in. The
goodbye plays at the car door on a delivery, not the moment you stop buying.

THE HUNT. Lamar comes along and is recorded. Lookouts instead of wind -- get
spotted and a phone call scares the quarry off -- and tracks you can actually
see.

LAMAR AND HAO CHANGE THEIR CLOTHES day to day. DJ Pooh is behind the decks at
Lamar's and Johnny Guns is on Stretch's door. The two Online models that were
standing in for people are out. Franklin holds a pistol sideways off the
wardrobe. Loot lets you pick what you take, and the card wears the set's
colour. The messages tile breathes red with something unread.

TWENTY-ONE FIXES FROM A FULL AUDIT. A failed hunt locked every mission for the
rest of the session, with no way back. The wanted ceiling could ratchet to one
star and never recover. A script reload destroyed whatever was in your dead
drop. Handing over 27g of weed paid double what 28g did. Lamar repeated his
whole introduction on every visit. Ninety-two feed reactions had never once
fired. Four natives were called with the wrong number of arguments. The car
meet had no teardown. The reply matcher answered "warrant" with war and
"Carson" with garage lines. The bed and the closet fought over the same metre
of floor. Cruise peds went back to the world deaf. And the two elements on
screen during ordinary play cost seventy-five rectangles a frame, which past
four hundred machine-wide is the HUD flicker -- they cost four now. The whole
list, with file and line, is on GitHub.

THE FEED TOGGLE IS F4. It was NumPad *, and Fumes reads that key at the pump.

SMALLER. One seal for the whole set of mods at load, and the stock start-up
tickers are gone. The panel art comes back after a screen fade instead of
drawing nothing. A line with no recording names its own file in the log, and
the to-record tool no longer lists phone texts or feed posts, which are read
and never spoken. Voice pack: 152 lines to 191.
