THE CAR MEET, OVERHAULED. Nobody warps and nobody stands doing nothing. Every
walk-over used to be a go-to task cancelled on the same frame by a scenario
with its teleport flag on, at the car's centre height -- waist deep in the
ground, then stood there with nothing -- and on a driver still in his seat it
played a standing scenario in the seat. Now everybody at the meet, driver,
passenger or walk-in, gets out, walks on the nav mesh and does the thing:
leaning into an engine bay, talking at a nose, filming down a side, a smoke,
a crouch at a wheel, the huddle, a whoop at the lowrider. Stints of twenty to
sixty seconds and never the same place twice. Walk-ins are put down out of
your sight. Cars slow to a crawl inside the lot, stop for people, go in one
at a time and are squared up if the park does not quite land. Most bring a
passenger, the lowrider carries the music, and at time the lids come down and
they pull out one by one.

LAMAR'S HUNT, REDONE. Same words, different job underneath them. The ride out
has a marker. Lamar rides in whatever you drive, follows at your pace,
crouches when you crouch and stands still when you aim. The three Ballas and
the lookouts spawn out of sight with their gang AI switched off, so a glance
is no longer a shootout. Each target has a purple search ring that comes off
when you stand on his trail; they walk between two spots and leave fresh
prints, and the prints are bigger and lighter. Lamar's call goes when you are
crouched and still with one of them out of sight, and brings him to a spot a
rifle's length short of you. And Lamar goes back to his corner afterwards --
he stayed lent for the session before, which is a man you cannot talk to.

YOU LOSE WHAT YOU ARE CARRYING WHEN YOU DIE. You did not. The drop-on-death
check lived inside a tick that stands down while you are dead, so it never
once saw a death -- or an arrest. Dying leaves your product in a bag where you
fell, blipped, for ten minutes; an arrest loses it for good; and [Risk]
DeathBagRecoverable=false makes death lose it for good too.

VERNON ACTS HIS LINES. A hand off the game's street-conversation set on every
spoken line, picked from the words; a full dance for every verse, for as long
as OG Vee is on the mic; the set thrown up when the verse ends. And the hot
takes are brought down to Gerald's level: [Voice] Level.<speaker> scales one
man's files under the main volume, measured over the whole pack -- Vernon's
eight decibels hotter, the verses five, Lamar's three.

THE CONVERSATION PANEL, CLEANED UP. One header row -- picture, name, place --
the words, one hairline, the choices, the keys. The wordmark, the cursive
headline, the white well behind the photograph and the plate, sheen and
glowing frame under the chosen line all go; the chosen line gets a soft fill
and a rail.

THE INCOMING CALL. Keys on the buttons -- A or ENTER on the green, B or
BACKSPACE on the red -- and A answers on a pad, where it was D-pad right. A
drain under the buttons shows how long he keeps ringing.

THE HOUSE, EXPLAINED ONCE. The first time through Denise's door a page comes
up explaining the kitchen, the bed, the closet and the stash.

THE MAP WAITS FOR GERALD, AND SO DOES HAO'S NUMBER. Hao's lot and Vernon's
razor blade went on a fresh save's map before Gerald had put anything in your
hand; both wait for his first front now. Hao's "from the lot" text arrived on
saves that had never been to the lot; talking to him there is what unlocks
the phone.

THE PORT RUN'S LOAD IS TWENTY KILOS. Four biker coke bales on the pallet was a
couple of hundred kilos behind a card that says 20kg. Single kilo bricks now,
one per kilo, most on the pallet when it comes out and the men lifting the
last four.

THE PAINT. The can sprays blood, never bullets: splatters_blood is the default
texture and the impact family is refused wherever it is asked for. Old tags
come back in seconds: the game takes about thirty-two new decals a frame and
the engine was asking for thousands at once, reading every refusal as a full
pool and wiping far paint to make room it could not use. It feeds the wall
twenty a frame now, nearest first, checks its marks are still alive, learns
the game's pool from where evictions start, and gives up the furthest mark
rather than the oldest. The record cap is a quarter of a million.
