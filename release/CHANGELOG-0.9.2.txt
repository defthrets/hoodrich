VERNON'S NUMBER IS THE REST OF THE PAY. The buy at Rogers Scrap ended with him
counting cash out in a yard and that was all of it -- you drove back to
Strawberry and he went and leaned on his wall again, with nothing about the
basement he had just shown you any different. A man whose stock you are the
reason he still has does not go back to being scenery.

So he texts you once you have gone. Powder, brought to the house, at a number
nobody else in the mod will do -- under the port, which was the cheapest thing
there was. What stops it being free money is the size: two kilos a call. He is
a man with a basement, not a dock, and bulk still comes off Tao Cheng.

Not at the hand-in. A phone going off in your pocket while its owner is stood
in front of you counting your cut is the mod talking over itself, so it waits
until the shop is a hundred and twenty metres behind you -- which is when
somebody who has had a think about it actually gets in touch.


THE TAKEOVER CARS STOP SKIDDING THROUGH THEIR OWN PITCH. They arrived flat
out: the run-in was one drive task at twelve metres a second held right up to
a five-metre arrival circle, and the first thing that happened on crossing it
was a brake -- forty kilometres an hour going into a stop with five metres to
do it in. Three things, and the last two are the same bug from either end.

  The last twenty metres are driven at five a second. A roll, not a crawl: he
  is still arriving, he is just not arriving AT the mark.

  The brake stays on grip. The slick rig and the drift tyres used to go on in
  the same breath as the brake, so the stop was taken on two fifths of the
  car's grip. They go on at the lock instead, where he is stationary and the
  change cannot be seen.

  And the smoke waits for him to actually be stopped. The settle was a fixed
  second and a half; if he was still rolling when it ran out, the standing
  burnout began on a car that was not standing and the donut inherited it.
